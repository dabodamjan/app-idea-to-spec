# ASO basics

App Store Optimization is how people find your app without you paying for every install. What actually drives discovery and downloads in 2026, in priority order.

ASO is not a one-time metadata fill-in. It is the foundation of sustainable organic growth, and it is continuous: research, ship, measure, iterate. Paid installs alone do not carry an app.

## The levers, in priority order

### 1. Title and subtitle keywords (ranking)

These matter most for ranking. Put your single most important keyword plus your brand in the title. A keyword-optimized title measurably lifts impressions. The iOS keyword field (100 characters) is the dedicated keyword surface. Rules that matter:

- Do not repeat words across the title, subtitle, and keyword field. Each word counts once, so repetition wastes the slot.
- Do not use competitor brand names.

### 2. Screenshots and icon (conversion)

If keywords get you seen, screenshots decide whether you get downloaded. The first two or three screenshots, the ones visible without scrolling, decide it in a few seconds. Lead with the core value and social proof (ratings, an editorial mention, download counts). Strong screenshots are one of the biggest conversion levers you have. The icon is the first impression of all.

### 3. Ratings, reviews, and engagement (now ranking signals)

Ratings, reviews, download velocity, and retention are ranking signals in 2026, not just trust signals. The store algorithms weigh engagement and retention, so a well-timed in-app review prompt and actually-retained users feed your discovery. Ask for the review at a moment of value, not on first launch.

### 4. Localization (high ROI, underused)

Localizing your metadata, and ideally your screenshots, for major markets unlocks ranking in those storefronts. iOS also indexes extra keywords across certain localizations even for an English app. Indies underuse this, which is exactly why it is an edge.

### 5. Custom Product Pages and in-store A/B testing

Product Page Optimization lets you test icons and screenshots, and custom product pages let you tailor the page to specific ad campaigns. This is the modern way to optimize conversion without shipping an app update.

## What this means for your spec

Section 12 captures an ASO starter, not a full campaign: a working title with your main keyword, a subtitle, and a keyword direction. You are not optimizing yet, you are making sure the app has a name and a positioning that can rank, and reserving the screenshot and keyword work as a known launch task rather than an afterthought. The product and its store presence ship together, so the spec should treat the icon, the first few screenshots, and the title as part of the MVP, not post-launch polish.
